Learn Prescriptive Analytics with Phoebe

Ravensworth Mills makes three feeds. Show earns 71 a tonne, nearly double Standard at 38, so the obvious move is to make more Show. The optimal plan makes none of it at all, because Show eats 2.3 milling hours a tonne against Standard's 1.1, and the mill is not allocating margin, it is allocating hours. That is the whole subject in one result: prediction tells you what will happen, and something else entirely tells you what to do about it. Six leader sessions on reading and challenging a plan, ten practitioner sessions building the solver that produces one, and every figure here computed by a real simplex implementation running in the page.

What is real here

The solver is a simplex implementation written from the method, with branch and bound over its relaxation. Every plan on every page is computed, not stored.

The solverCarries three problems whose optima can be checked by hand, and runs them on load
And it was wrongPassing those three was not enough. A cross-check over 1,100 generated problems found two faults in opposite directions, one in the solver and one in the checker
Every shadow priceQuoted with the range it holds over, because 33.79 an hour stops being true after 0.46 of an hour
Every planRun against drawn demand as well as average demand, because those give different answers
